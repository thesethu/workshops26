How the rules work

Making laws: the House and Senate must pass the same bill before it goes to the president. The president can sign it or veto it. Congress can override a veto only with a two-thirds vote in both chambers.

Senate filibuster: most contested legislation needs three-fifths of senators, normally 60 when all 100 seats are filled, to end debate. Certain tax and spending measures can use the separate budget reconciliation process.

Appointments: the president nominates federal judges, Cabinet secretaries and many other senior officials, but the Senate decides whether to confirm them.

Executive power: presidents can act without a new law in some areas, but those actions remain subject to existing laws, congressional funding decisions and review by the courts.

A 50–50 Senate: the vice-president can break tie votes. Independent senators also affect control depending on which party they caucus with.
